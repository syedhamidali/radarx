# Data tables of the drop size distribution modules

This note documents `dsd_scattering.csv` and `dsd_prior_perils2022.csv`
(provenance, calculation, citations). The CSV files themselves are unchanged
(their loaders skip a fixed number of header lines, so the description is kept here).
`ziegler2013_profiles_cm1_squall_line.csv` is documented in its own header
and in `radarx.retrieve.diabatic_lagrangian`.

## `dsd_scattering.csv`

Single-drop scattering of raindrops, read by `radarx.retrieve.dsd`
(`scattering_table`, `dsd`, `radar_from_dsd`) and `radarx.retrieve.dsd_bayes`.

* Provenance: generated by `ci/build_dsd_tables.py` (run it to regenerate; it
  needs `pytmatrix`, which radarx itself does not).
* Calculation: T-matrix method (Mishchenko and Travis 1998,
  doi:10.1016/S0022-4073(98)00008-9) through the `pytmatrix` interface
  (Leinonen 2014, doi:10.1364/OE.22.001655), backscattering and forward
  scattering of an orientation-averaged oblate spheroid, per equal-volume
  diameter 0.05-8 mm in steps of 0.05 mm, at 2.8, 5.6 and 9.4 GHz (S, C, X
  band; radarx choices of representative frequencies; wavelengths 107.07,
  53.53, 31.89 mm) and 0, 10, 20 and 30 degrees Celsius.
* Axis ratio: Brandes et al. (2002), doi:10.1175/1520-0450(2002)041<0674:EIREWA>2.0.CO;2,
  b/a = 0.9951 + 0.0251 D - 0.03644 D^2 + 0.005303 D^3 - 0.0002492 D^4 (D in
  mm), capped at 1. The polynomial agrees with Eq. 15 of Kumjian and Ryzhkov
  (2010, doi:10.1175/2010JAMC2243.1), which prints the second coefficient as
  0.025 10. The equation number in Brandes et al. is not checked.
* Canting: Gaussian, zero mean, 7 degrees standard deviation, attributed to
  Huang et al. (2008), doi:10.1175/2008JTECHA1075.1 (value not checked
  against the paper).
* Refractive index of water: the Debye-type fit of Ray (1972),
  doi:10.1364/AO.11.001836 (constants not checked against the
  paper); the Z_H normalization in
  `radarx.retrieve.dsd` uses |K_w|^2 = 0.93 for every band and temperature.
* Geometry: **horizontal incidence** (0 degrees elevation). ZDR of oblate
  drops decreases with elevation (about 12 % lower at 20 degrees in a Rayleigh
  estimate, see the warning in `radarx.retrieve.dsd`), so the table should not
  be applied to QVPs or sweeps above about 5 degrees without a correction.
* Columns: `sigma_h`, `sigma_v` (backscatter cross sections, mm2),
  `copol_re`, `copol_im` (copolar correlation terms, mm2), `kdp`
  (degrees/km per drop per m3), `ah`, `av` (dB/km per drop per m3).

## `dsd_prior_perils2022.csv`

Gamma DSD parameters used by `radarx.retrieve.dsd_prior("perils2022")` as the
learned prior of `radarx.retrieve.dsd_bayesian`.

* Provenance: 1-min spectra of the Portable In situ Precipitation Stations
  (OTT Parsivel2) of the PERiLS 2022 field campaign (IOP1 22 March, IOP2
  30-31 March, IOP3 5 April 2022; northern Mississippi and Alabama),
  matched to the lowest WSR-88D beam over each probe along the drop fall
  trajectories (see `radarx.retrieve.rain_trajectories`), built by
  `ml/models/bayesian_dsd/prior_samples.py` (not part of the package). The
  file holds derived gamma parameters, not the raw spectra. The prior was
  learned from the PIPS spectra of the data set below, and no quality
  control beyond the processing of the drop size distributions in that data
  set was applied.
* Data: Dawson, D., M. Biggerstaff, and S. Waugh, 2025: PERiLS_2022:
  Portable In Situ Precipitation Stations (PIPS) Data. Version 1.0. NSF NCAR
  Earth Observing Laboratory, https://doi.org/10.26023/HFBG-7W5M-WA00. The
  data are distributed under the UCAR terms of use
  (https://www.ucar.edu/terms-of-use).
* Campaign: Kosiba, K. A., and Coauthors, 2024: The Propagation, Evolution,
  and Rotation in Linear Storms (PERiLS) Project. Bull. Amer. Meteor. Soc.,
  105, E1768-E1799, https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-22-0064.1.
* Acknowledgement: we thank the PERiLS investigators and the NSF NCAR Earth
  Observing Laboratory for these data. The default prior of radarx is
  `"generic"`, which does not depend on them.
* Fit: the 2-4-6 method of moments (Cao and Zhang 2009,
  doi:10.1175/2008JAMC2026.1) for mu; Dm and Nw from the moments (Testud et
  al. 2001, doi:10.1175/1520-0450(2001)040<1118:TCONDT>2.0.CO;2); minutes with
  R >= 0.5 mm/h and at least 50 drops per m3 (a radarx selection).
* Columns: `log10_nw` (log10 of m-3 mm-1), `dm` (mm), `mu`, `iop`.
* Scope: one campaign (quasi-linear convective systems in the southeastern
  United States); it is a regional prior, not a climatology. The
  corresponding approach of Cao et al. (2010,
  doi:10.1175/2009JAMC2227.1) builds its prior from 30 000 min of Oklahoma
  2DVD data.
