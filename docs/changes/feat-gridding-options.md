- **FIX:** The grids of `grid_cones`, `grid_radars` and the Cartesian `create_cappi` are georeferenced as CF when they are built, with one shared builder (`radarx._gridcf`) that `write_netcdf_cf` also uses. `lat` and `lon` are now 2-D on `(y, x)` computed from the grid's azimuthal equidistant CRS (the 1-D `lat(y)` and `lon(x)` of `grid_cones` were 633 m off in the corner of a grid 120 km wide and 9 km off in the corner of one 460 km wide; the new ones agree with the geodesic to 1 cm), every field with `x` and `y` has `grid_mapping = "crs_wkt"`, and `x`, `y`, `z` and `time` have `standard_name`, `units`, `axis` and `positive`; cf_xarray and rioxarray find the CRS. `create_cappi` had no CRS; it now has the one centred on the radar, which is what xradar's `x` and `y` are. Code that used `grid.lat` as a vector along `y` must use `grid.lat.sel(x=0)` or the 2-D arrays. Files written by `write_netcdf_cf` do not change. ({issue}`265`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ADD:** `grid_gates` and `grid_rhi` (and `.radarx.grid_gates`, `.radarx.grid_rhi`) grid radar gates with a radius of influence in metres: the weighted mean of the gates within the radius of each output point, with the Cressman (1959) weight `(R^2 - d^2) / (R^2 + d^2)`, the Barnes (1964) weight `exp(-4 d^2 / R^2)` or the nearest gate. The radius is constant, grows with the distance from the radar and the height (`roi_range_factor`, `roi_height_factor`, the `xy_factor` and `z_factor` of Py-ART) or follows the beam width (`beam_width`, the `dist_beam` term of Py-ART). A gate is a point in space, so the same call grids full volumes, sector scans, RHIs and volumes with repeated low tilts, which `motion` moves to the analysis time before they are averaged; `grid_rhi` grids a vertical section through the radar at an azimuth. A multithreaded C++ kernel (gates sorted into buckets) does the work, with a NumPy and SciPy fallback that gives the same numbers; 2.7 million cells from 14 sweeps take 0.5 s. On synthetic fields with known truth the rms error is 0.06 to 0.14 for a plane wave and a Gaussian blob and 0.8 to 1.6 for a field that is linear in height (cone gridding reproduces it exactly), where gates on one side only at the edge of the data bias the mean. ({issue}`238`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ADD:** `grid_cones(..., repeats=)` merges sweeps of one elevation: `"mean"` averages the repeats that have a value at a cell, `"latest"` and `"nearest"` take the latest repeat or the one closest to `time` that has a value, so the split cuts of NEXRAD and the extra low tilts of SAILS and MESO-SAILS are used and not dropped. With `motion` every repeat is moved to the analysis time first. The default `"farthest"` keeps the current numbers. ({issue}`238`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
