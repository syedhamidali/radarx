- **ADD:** `forward_operator` computes the horizontal reflectivity `DBZH`, `ZDR`, `KDP`, `RHOHV` and the reflectivity-weighted fall speed of a numerical model state on the model grid, and `to_radar_grid` samples them along the beam of a radar (4/3 Earth radius with a `k_e` argument and the site altitude in the Earth radius, inverse-distance neighbour columns, linear in height) with the radial velocity `VRADH` from the model wind and the fall speed, for one elevation or a volume (a `DataTree`). `wrf_state` turns a WRF `wrfout` Dataset (destaggered winds and heights, temperature from `T`, `P`, `PB`, with the moist potential temperature if `USE_THETA_M` is set) and `mpas_state` an MPAS history or diagnostics Dataset on its unstructured mesh (with the grid file for `lonCell`, `latCell`, `zgrid`) into the standard state; the horizontal search is a KD-tree on the sphere, so the curvilinear WRF grid and the MPAS mesh use the same call. Rain uses the T-matrix scattering tables of the DSD retrieval (S, C and X band, the table temperature from the model temperature) and agrees with `radar_from_dsd` to rounding; the gamma size distribution comes from the rain mixing ratio with a fixed intercept `n0_rain` and shape `mu_rain`, or from the number concentration (`QNRAIN`). Snow, hail and graupel are dry exponential size distributions with Rayleigh scattering by oblate spheroids with a Gaussian canting angle, from the equations of Ryzhkov et al. (2020, Appendix A and Eqs. 3-5) and the shapes, densities, intercepts and orientations of Jung et al. (2008) (their dry-snow and dry-hail amplitudes are reproduced to 2.6 % and 0.4 %); graupel has no preset and needs `graupel_params`. Not included, and said so in the documentation: the melting classes (the paper does not give the size distribution of the mixture), attenuation, the beam pattern, non-zero elevation in the orientation moments, the rain size distributions of the double-moment schemes (the scheme relations could not be read, so the rain assumption is an argument), and the ice fall speed (a constant per class, 0 by default). Ryzhkov et al. (2011) was not read (no open copy); its equations are taken from the 2020 review. No WRF output could be read on the development machine, so the operator is tested on synthetic WRF-like and MPAS-like Datasets built from the Registries of the two models, against closed forms (the Rayleigh limit of exponential rain, the closed forms of the paper for ice) and `radar_from_dsd`. A compiled multithreaded kernel (per cell and per gate) with a NumPy fallback that gives the same numbers; lazy dask arrays, the `.radarx.forward_operator` and `.radarx.to_radar_grid` accessor methods and the new notebook `Forward_Operator`.
