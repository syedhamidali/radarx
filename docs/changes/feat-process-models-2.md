- **ADD:** `radarx.fundamentals.trace_rays` integrates the ray of the radar beam through a profile of the radio refractivity (Snell's law in a spherically stratified atmosphere, Bean and Dutton 1966, Eqs. 1.27 and 3.1) and gives the true beam height, ground range and ray elevation of every gate as an option next to the 4/3 Earth model; `k_e_from_rays` turns a traced beam into the effective Earth radius factor that `grid_radar`, the merging functions and `beam_blockage` take as `k_e`, and `radarx.retrieve.beam_path` (also `.radarx.beam_path`) puts the tracer on the gates of a sweep or a volume from a sounding or reanalysis profile, with `beam_height`, `ground_range`, `gate_elevation` and `k_e` as products. A compiled multithreaded kernel (over elevations, fourth-order Runge-Kutta with steps that end at the levels of the profile) with a NumPy twin does the work; dask-lazy, the input is never changed. For the 4/3 Earth gradient (-39.2 N-units per km) it reproduces the effective radius result to 0.5 m in height and 1.6e-4 in ground range up to 200 km and 1 degree elevation, and it matches the closed form of a profile whose rays are logarithmic spirals to 4 mm, an independent layer-by-layer SciPy integration to 0.2 mm and Snell's invariant to 5e-9; sub-refractive profiles bend the beam up, super-refractive ones down and a layer steeper than -157 N-units per km traps it. The profile is linear between its levels and constant outside, and the range is the path length (radarx choices); the new notebook `Beam_Refraction`. ({issue}`239`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ADD:** `radarx.io.sounding.refractivity` computes the radio refractivity from pressure, temperature and vapour pressure with the two-term formula of Bean and Dutton (1966, Eq. 1.17, constants of Smith and Weintraub 1953, read in the book), with the three-term form and the constants of ITU-R P.453-9 as options, in the compiled thermodynamics kernel with its NumPy twin. ({issue}`239`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ADD:** `rain_shaft` (also `.radarx.rain_shaft` on a DSD) evolves the drop size distribution of a column of still air with collisions, coalescence and breakup, the collisional part that `rain_trajectories` cannot carry: the geometric collision kernel, the coalescence efficiency of Low and List (1982a, Eqs. 4.5-4.6) and their breakup fractions, fragment numbers and small-fragment distributions (1982b), a large remnant that conserves the mass of each event (as McFarquhar 2004) and fixed pivots that conserve number and mass, integrated with Runge-Kutta in the fall distance with the fall-speed laws and density correction of the trajectory model. The mass of every event and the rain rate of the shaft are conserved to rounding (1e-14, 1e-11), the numbers of Tables 1 to 3 of the two papers (efficiencies, breakup fractions, fragment numbers) are reproduced to the printed digits, the compiled multithreaded kernel (over the event tensor and over columns) and its NumPy twin agree to 1e-13, and the integration agrees with SciPy. Four printed equations of Part II could not be read literally and are listed in the documentation (the sign of the 0.170 of Eq. 3.3, which Table 1 fixes, `D_S0`, the weights of Eq. 4.20 and the exponent of Eq. 4.27); the Gaussian large remnants of the paper are replaced by the mass-conserving drop, the fragment distributions are extrapolations outside the experiments (large drops 1.8 to 4.6 mm, small drops 0.395 to 1.8 mm) and drop collisions use an efficiency of one. McFarquhar's (2004) fragment distributions (the lower size limit of its log-normal parts is not given in the text, and one coefficient of its Table 2 disagrees with its Fig. 10), the bulk scheme of Seifert et al. (2005) and the collision efficiencies of Hall (1980) were not available and are not implemented; the new notebook `Rain_Collisions`. ({issue}`239`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ADD:** `hail_fall_speed` (also `.radarx.hail_fall_speed`) gives the terminal velocity of hail from its maximum dimension with the size relations of the corrigendum of Heymsfield et al. (2018, corrigendum 2020: 7.6 D^0.89 and 8.4 D^0.67 for D below and above 1.576 cm, the single law of the observations and the 10th to 90th percentile laws), `melting_snow_fall_speed` (also `.radarx.melting_snow_fall_speed`) mixes the speed of the dry snowflake and of the raindrop of the same mass with the weight of Frick et al. (2013, Eqs. 14 and 15, after Mitra et al. 1990), and both are options of the trajectory model, `rain_trajectories(..., fall_speed=("hail", law))` and `fall_speed=("melting_snow", liquid_fraction, a, b)`, with the kernel and its NumPy twin extended and the existing defaults unchanged. The hail laws are fits for stones above 5 mm (a warning below), neither law has a density correction because their sources give none, and evaporation must be off for them; the dry-snow law of the melting snow has no default because the relation of Frick et al. (Khvorostyanov and Curry 2005) could not be read. The hail laws of Knight and Heymsfield (1983) and Heymsfield and Wright (2014) could not be read and are not implemented.
