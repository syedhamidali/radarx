- **FIX:** `gbvtd` fits the Fourier series up to the order 4 (`max_wavenumber=4` is the default, nine coefficients as in Lee et al. 1999, Eqs. 8 and 19 to 27, and Lee et al. 2000) and takes `A4` and `B4` into the along-beam mean wind (`A0 + A2 + A4`) and the wavenumber-1 wind (`-2 (B2 + B4)`, `A2 - A0 + A4 + (A0 + A2 + A4) cos(alpha_max)`). A series that stops at the order 3 got these wrong whenever there was a wavenumber-3 asymmetry: the wavenumber-1 cosine part by the amplitude of the wavenumber 3 and the along-beam wind by half of it, 10 and 5 m/s for the 10 m/s asymmetry of the test vortex. The wavenumber-3 tangential wind is a result now (`VT3_COS`, `VT3_SIN`, `VT3_AMPLITUDE`); a ring needs a data gap of at most 30 degrees for the order 4 (the orders 3 and 4 have the same limit, so `max_wavenumber=3` still works as before). The default order of `gbvtd` was 3, so numbers of `gbvtd` change slightly; `tc_center` keeps the order 2 (the wavenumbers 0 and 1 of the vortex), whose maximum winds are the ones of Table 4 of Lee and Marks (2000). by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)

- **ADD:** `tc_center(selection="max_vt")` takes the ring with the highest axisymmetric tangential wind as the radius of maximum wind and the centre, as Lee and Marks (2000) do; `"norm"` (default) is the NORM score of Bell and Lee (2012). `gbvtd(geometric_limit=True)` and `tc_center(geometric_limit=True)` limit the Fourier order of a ring by `sin(alpha_max) = R / R_T` so that the wavenumbers 1, 2 and 3 are fitted only up to 0.7, 0.5 and 0.33 (the limits of a 20 percent error of Lee et al. 1999, applied in Lee et al. 2000); the compiled kernel and the NumPy twin agree. by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
