- **ADD:** `azimuthal_shear_layer_max` (and `.radarx.azimuthal_shear_layer_max`) computes the layer-maximum azimuthal shear of the MRMS rotation products from a volume: at every gate of a reference tilt (the lowest by default) the largest LLSD shear of the tilts whose beam centre is inside a height layer (`layer=(0, 2000)` or `(3000, 6000)` m above the radar, 4/3 Earth model) at the same ground range and azimuth, with the elevation angle of the tilt that gave it. The test of Smith et al. (2016) that the shear is kept only in or near a storm cell is `min_reflectivity=20` within `reflectivity_distance=5000` m (the reflectivity may come from the surveillance cut of a split cut), `rotation` selects the cyclonic maximum, the anticyclonic minimum or the largest magnitude, and `shear=` takes shear fields already in the volume. Compiled multithreaded kernels (`layer_max`, `near_mask`) with a NumPy fallback that gives the same values. Against a vortex column whose rotation changes with height the layer maxima are 0.00422 and 0.01169 s-1 for analytic 0.00421 and 0.01171. Against the MRMS 0-2 km product on the KGWX squall line of 30 March 2022 (four volumes, eight MRMS times, cells 20-100 km from the radar) the median pointwise correlation rises from 0.34 for the 0.5 degree tilt to 0.42 for the layer maximum, and the median ratio of the maxima in 3 km blocks (radarx over MRMS) from 0.60 to 0.92. Miller et al. (2013) could not be read and is not used. ({issue}`255`)
- **ENH:** `llsd`, `azimuthal_shear`, `radial_divergence` and the layer maximum have a `median_filter` option, the 3 x 3 median filter that Smith and Elmore (2004) apply to the velocity before the fit (`True` or an odd window size; rays wrap around north). The default is no filter, so the numbers do not change. The preprint, which was read in full this time and not only as an abstract, does not say how missing gates enter the median: a gate keeps a value only if it is valid itself and its window holds more than half of its gates (a radarx rule). Compiled kernel with a NumPy fallback. On the KGWX comparison with MRMS it raises the median pointwise correlation of the layer maximum from 0.42 to 0.46 and lowers the block-maximum ratio from 0.92 to 0.71. ({issue}`255`)
- **ENH:** `rotation_couplets(volume, min_tilts=N)` keeps the couplets that are on a chain of at least N tilts: from each couplet the nearest couplet of the same sense of rotation within `link_distance` (5 km) of the peak is followed up and down the elevation angles, with `max_skip` missing tilts allowed, after the association of shear features across elevation angles in the algorithm description of the NSSL Mesocyclone Detection Algorithm (the distance and the nearest-couplet choice are radarx's). `n_tilts` is added to the couplets. `median_filter` is passed to the LLSD. The defaults keep every couplet and its numbers. ({issue}`255`)
- **ADD:** `correct_dual_prf` (and `.radarx.correct_dual_prf`) corrects the isolated dealiasing errors of dual-PRF radial velocities with the local dealiasing step of Holleman and Beekhuis (2003): a gate that differs from the median of at least nine valid gates of its window (3 x 3, enlarged to 9 x 9 if needed) by more than the unambiguous velocity of its ray's PRF is moved by a multiple of twice that velocity. The rays' PRF comes from `prf` or alternates. On synthetic dual-PRF data (high and low Nyquist velocities of 16 and 12 m/s, noise 1 m/s) 0.46 % of the gates are outliers before and none after one pass. The group-size filter and the global dealiasing of the paper, and the PRF reconstruction, are not implemented; Joe and May (2003) could not be read. Compiled median kernel with a NumPy fallback. ({issue}`257`)
- **ADD:** `dealias_confidence` (and `.radarx.dealias_confidence`) and `dealias_velocity(..., confidence=True)` give the share of the valid neighbouring gates (3 x 3) that differ from a gate by no more than the Nyquist velocity, that is that prefer its fold to the next one; 1 in a smooth field, 5/8 on both sides of a straight fold line, 0 for an isolated gate unfolded differently from all its neighbours. It is a radarx definition (no paper defines one) computed from the result alone, so it also applies to other dealiasing methods. The default of `dealias_velocity` is unchanged. Compiled kernel with a NumPy fallback. On the 0.5 degree cut of the KGWX volume of 23:46 UTC 99.3 % of the gates have confidence 1 and 0.04 % are below 0.5. ({issue}`257`)
- **ADD:** `dealias_velocity(..., method="unravel")` implements the UNRAVEL algorithm of Louf et al. (2020) from the paper (read in full; the authors' open-source code was used to cross-check the order of the steps and the values the paper does not give, and no code was taken from it): the modified z-score pre-processing with a first unfolding, the two reference radials, azimuthal continuity in four sectors, two passes of range and azimuthal continuity with a growing window, the four box passes, the closest-reference module, the least-squares and box checks and, for a volume, the check against the sweep below (`sweep_continuity`), with `alpha=0.8` (0.6 in the first pass). It needs no wind profile or reference. Left out: the long-range driver of the paper, the box that precedes the gate and the reflectivity gate filter; the paper does not give the pre-processing tolerance, the second window, the box around the closest reference, the box of the check and the windows of the 3-D check, so radarx chose them (the module documentation lists them). A compiled kernel (sweeps in parallel) and a Python specification give identical values. The default `method="region"` is unchanged. On Doppler cuts of four KGWX volumes folded to a Nyquist velocity of 13.3 m/s the error rate against the unfolded cuts is 1-22 % for the region method and 4-39 % for UNRAVEL (29-58 % without the check between sweeps): better on two volumes and worse on two, and not the 0.2 % of the paper's test. ({issue}`257`)
