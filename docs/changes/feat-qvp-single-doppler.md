- **ADD:** `rd_qvp`, `cvp` and `rhi_qvp` build vertical profiles without a high sweep. `rd_qvp` is the range-defined QVP of Tobin and Kumjian (2017), as described by Murphy et al. (2020) because the paper is not open access: the azimuthal means of all sweeps within `rmax` (50 km) of the radar combined on a height grid with Cressman weights times `ground_range ** -power` (the form of the weight is radarx's own, `power=2`, not checked against the paper). `cvp` is the columnar vertical profile of Murphy et al. (2020): a sector of `half_range` and `half_azimuth` around a point, each sweep used only between the beams half-way to its neighbours at the centre, and Cressman averaging (100 m radius, 50 m grid, dB averaged in dB as in the paper). `rhi_qvp` converts the range of an RHI to height and averages the gates of all elevations in 100 m bins with the settings of Allabakash et al. (2019) (rhohv > 0.6, Z >= 0 dBZ, 6 to 20 km, below 45 degrees). All three accept a sequence of volumes for a time-height profile, use a compiled multithreaded kernel (`height_blend`) with a NumPy fallback, and have `.radarx.rd_qvp`, `.radarx.cvp` and `.radarx.rhi_qvp` methods. Left out: the exact weights of Tobin and Kumjian (2017), the moving CVP and the melting-layer detection of Allabakash et al. (2019) from gradients (use `melting_layer` on the profile). ({issue}`256`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ADD:** `melting_layer_ppi` finds the melting layer on the PPIs of a volume (4 to 10 degrees) with the radial-profile rules of Giangrande et al. (2008): gates with 0.90 < rhohv < 0.97 below 6 km whose 500 m window above has Z of 30 to 47 dBZ and ZDR of 0.8 to 2.5 dB, and the 80th and 20th percentile of their heights in a 20 degree running azimuth window (the window of the conference version of the paper, the percentiles as described by Ryzhkov and Krause 2022), for every azimuth and for all azimuths together, with a compiled kernel and a NumPy fallback; the new notebook sections compare it with the QVP layer (KGWX 31 March 2022, S band: PPI top 78 m above the QVP onset top on average; ARMOR, C band, convective: top medians within 16 m, bottom 556 m lower). The beam-broadening correction of Ryzhkov and Krause (2022), the weighting of elevations and the temporal logic of the operational algorithm are not implemented. `.radarx.melting_layer_ppi` is the accessor method. ({issue}`256`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ADD:** `mask_above_melting_layer` (and `.radarx.mask_above_melting_layer`) sets rain-only products (`RAIN_RATE*`, `N0`, `NW`, `D0`, `DM`, `MU`, `LAMBDA`, `LWC`, or `variables=`) to NaN above the bottom of the melting layer from `melting_layer` or `melting_layer_ppi` (or a number), with a `margin` and an optional `beam_width` that uses the lower edge of the beam; the layer may depend on the azimuth, gates are kept where it is unknown, `RAIN_RATE_FLAG` becomes 4 (`above_melting_layer`, the flags of `rain_rate` plus one) where an estimate was removed, dask arrays stay lazy and the input is not changed. ({issue}`256`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ADD:** `single_doppler_winds(uncertainty=True)` adds `u_uncertainty`, `v_uncertainty` and `w_uncertainty`: the standard deviation over `members` retrievals (network and refinement included) from inputs with random errors, `vr_error` on every gridded radial velocity and a smooth, mass-continuous `background_error` field of scales `correlation` on the background wind. It is a Monte Carlo propagation of the stated input errors, radarx's own definition and not the posterior covariance of the cost function; the weak continuity and smoothness terms are not perturbed. With weights that agree with the stated errors the RMS actual error is on average 1.36 (u), 1.17 (v) and 1.05 (w) times the RMS spread over 8 synthetic fields (single fields 0.8 to 1.6); with the default weights (background 0.001) the actual error is about twice the spread. ({issue}`258`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
- **ENH:** `single_doppler_winds` records in the attribute `fall_speed_correction` how the fall speed was handled. The variational retrieval and the refinement of a network prediction correct the radial velocity explicitly; the raw network prediction (`refine=False`, `u_network`, `v_network`, `w_network`) does not: the network takes the uncorrected velocity and learns the correction from the reflectivity, so `fall_speed_correction` has no effect on it, and `fall_speed_correction=False` without refinement now warns. The numbers of the retrievals are unchanged. A uniform wind in 45 dBZ rain is recovered exactly with the correction and with errors of 1.9, 2.0 and 0.9 m/s in u, v and w without it. ({issue}`258`) by [@syedhamidali](https://github.com/syedhamidali)
